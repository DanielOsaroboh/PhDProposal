# 10. Alignment with My Background

This proposed research sits at the intersection of predictive analytics, engineering systems, and petroleum engineering. My academic background, which includes a Master of Research in Artificial Intelligence with Distinction, provides a strong foundation in Bayesian optimisation, uncertainty quantification, and time-series data modelling. These capabilities are relevant to the computational components of the proposed research, particularly the prediction of foam rheology and stability, assessment of model uncertainty, and identification of candidate operating windows.

Professionally, my experience as a Research Assistant on a UKRI-funded Knowledge Transfer Partnership, where I contributed to the development of IoT-enabled predictive monitoring systems for high-pressure subsea hydraulic networks, provides relevant experience in analysing complex engineering systems involving pressure, operating variability, monitoring, and uncertainty. Although this experience is not equivalent to petroleum engineering or hydraulic fracturing-fluid design, it provides a transferable engineering systems perspective for investigating fluid behaviour and predictive performance under demanding operating conditions. My membership of the Society of Petroleum Engineers (SPE) further reflects my ongoing engagement with the petroleum and energy sector.

At the same time, I recognise that petroleum engineering, foam rheology, and hydraulic fracturing-fluid design are specialised areas in which I will need to deepen my knowledge during the early stages of candidature. My existing strengths lie primarily in artificial intelligence, predictive modelling, uncertainty quantification, and engineering systems. The doctoral programme will therefore require deliberate development of the domain-specific knowledge needed to apply these capabilities appropriately to nanoparticle-stabilised energised foam systems under HPHT conditions.

This interdisciplinary development is an important part of the proposed research and will help ensure that the computational methods are informed by relevant physical principles, experimental evidence, and petroleum engineering judgement.
