# 5. Aim, Objectives, and Research Questions

## 5.1 Aim

The primary aim of this doctoral research is to develop an uncertainty-aware predictive framework to support High-Pressure, High-Temperature (HPHT) energised foam design, utilising machine learning to predict apparent viscosity and stability, quantify uncertainty, and identify safe operating windows for hydraulic fracturing applications**.**

## 5.2 Research Questions

To achieve this aim, the research seeks to address the following technical questions:

1. Which formulation and operating variables most strongly control the apparent viscosity and foam stability of nanoparticle-stabilised energised foams under HPHT conditions?
2. How accurately can machine learning models predict apparent viscosity and foam stability across varying pressure, temperature, salinity, foam quality, and chemical formulation conditions?
3. How can prediction uncertainty be quantified and incorporated into the interpretation of foam rheology models?
4. How can uncertainty-aware predictions support operating-window identification for fracturing-fluid design and proppant transport assessment?

## 5.3 Research Objectives

The objectives of this research align directly with the research questions and the proposed methodology. Each objective is designed to yield a clear engineering deliverable:

To investigate the influence of pressure, temperature, fluid composition, and shear conditions on the rheological behaviour of energized foams under HPHT reservoir conditions.

1. Compile and structure HPHT foam rheology and stability datasets from available experimental and literature sources.
2. Develop and compare selected ML models for predicting apparent viscosity and foam stability indicators.
3. Quantify prediction uncertainty and assess model reliability under sparse or extrapolated HPHT conditions.
4. Identify operating windows for nanoparticle-stabilised energised foam formulations under defined reservoir and treatment constraints.
5. Evaluate the engineering implications of the model outputs for fracturing-fluid design and proppant transport
