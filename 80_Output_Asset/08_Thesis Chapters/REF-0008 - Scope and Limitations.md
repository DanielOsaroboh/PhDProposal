# 9. Scope and Limitations

This research is bounded to the evaluation and prediction of nanoparticle-stabilised energised fracturing foams under HPHT conditions. In direct alignment with the research objectives, a clear distinction is maintained between primary predictive targets and downstream engineering applications. The machine learning framework is scoped to predict apparent viscosity and foam stability, operationalised primarily through foam half-life, as the central predictive targets (Gonzalez Perdomo and Wan Madihi, 2022). Proppant transport assessment and operating-window identification are treated as downstream engineering applications informed by these predictions (Tran et al., 2025).

While the study will evaluate the engineering implications of foam rheology and stability for proppant suspension and transport, it will not attempt to develop a fully coupled three-dimensional hydraulic fracturing simulator. Instead, the framework will identify uncertainty-bounded candidate operating windows to support further laboratory evaluation, engineering assessment, and existing fracture-design workflows. The empirical modelling is also constrained by the availability, size, heterogeneity, variable coverage, and formulation specificity of accessible HPHT foam rheology and stability datasets (Al-Darweesh et al., 2024).
