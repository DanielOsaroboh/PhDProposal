Hydraulic fracturing operations in high-pressure, high-temperature (HPHT) reservoirs require stable energised foams with predictable rheological behaviour to ensure effective proppant transport and fracture conductivity. However, conventional empirical approaches are constrained by limited experimental data, complex nonlinear fluid behaviour, and the absence of reliable methods for identifying optimal operating conditions.

This research proposes an uncertainty-aware machine learning framework to predict the rheological properties of HPHT energised foams and identify engineering operating windows for hydraulic fracturing-fluid design.

The study integrates experimental data compilation, predictive modelling, uncertainty quantification, optimisation, and engineering assessment within a petroleum engineering research methodology. Machine learning serves as a decision-support tool rather than the primary focus, ensuring that engineering judgement remains central throughout the investigation.

The expected outcome is a robust predictive framework that improves engineering decision-making, supports hydraulic fracturing-fluid formulation under HPHT conditions, and provides a foundation for future laboratory validation and field-scale application, thereby contributing to both petroleum engineering practice and research.
