# HUB-T06 - Models

## Purpose

This HUB collects knowledge objects that answer:

> **How can the phenomenon be represented mathematically or empirically?**

A model is a mathematical, empirical, computational, or conceptual representation used to describe or predict system behaviour.

## Model Pattern

```text
Observed Phenomenon
        ↓
Assumptions
        ↓
Variables + Parameters
        ↓
Mathematical / Computational Representation
        ↓
Predicted Response
```

## Purpose

The model provides a mathematical representation of nonlinear  
shear-dependent viscosity.

Within foam rheology, it can provide a useful empirical representation  
of apparent-viscosity behaviour across changing shear conditions.

## Strength

Compared with a simple Power-law relationship, the model can represent  
transitions between viscosity regimes rather than assuming one  
power-law relationship across the complete shear-rate domain.

## Limitation under HPHT Conditions

The model remains dependent on fitted parameters.

Those parameters may reflect the particular:

- pressure;
- temperature;
- foam quality;
- formulation;
- salinity;
- gas characteristics;
- experimental conditions

under which they were obtained.

Therefore:

Good rheological representation  
≠  
Universal HPHT transferability

## Research Relevance

The Carreau model belongs to the conventional modelling baseline against  
which more flexible nonlinear predictive approaches can be understood.

The research problem is not that such models lack value.

The problem is whether their fitted relationships remain sufficiently  
reliable across changing and coupled HPHT conditions.
