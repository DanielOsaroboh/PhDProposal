# Martins Knowledge Formation and Asset Taxonomy

## Root Taxonomy

| Asset Class | Epistemic State | Primary Function | Core Verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| Processing Assets | Knowledge in formation | Transform | Process |
| Knowledge Assets | Formalised knowledge | Preserve and transfer | Know |
| Application Assets | Knowledge configured for use | Operationalise | Apply |
| Output Assets | Knowledge configured for audience | Communicate | Express |
| Meta-System | Knowledge governing knowledge | Control | Govern |

## Processing Asset Taxonomy

### External Knowledge
Epistemic material originating outside the system owner.

- **Source** — original external information object.
- **Source Note** — structured record of what one source says.
- **Literature Note** — concept- or problem-centred interpretation derived from external knowledge.
- **Source Synthesis** — integration of multiple external sources around a defined subject or problem.

### Internal Knowledge
Epistemic objects originating from observation, experience, reflection, reasoning, or internally generated questions.

- **Observation** — recorded noticed phenomenon.
- **Preliminary Interpretive Analysis** — provisional structured interpretation.
- **Decomposition** — separation into constituent analytical units.
- **Inquiry** — selected, framed, bounded knowledge problem.
- **Investigation** — evidence-seeking and reasoning process.
- **Discovery** — articulated new or significantly clarified understanding.
- **Knowledge Extraction** — transformation of situated discovery into candidate transferable knowledge.

## Knowledge Asset Taxonomy

- **Theory** — explanatory system of constructs and propositions.
- **Principle** — generalised governing proposition.
- **Meta-Framework** — higher-order structure for selecting, comparing, or coordinating frameworks.
- **Framework** — structured arrangement for organising understanding or analysis.
- **Concept** — defined mental abstraction representing a phenomenon or idea.
- **Mechanism** — causal or generative account of how one condition contributes to another.
- **Model** — simplified useful representation.
- **Pattern** — recurring configuration, sequence, or relationship.
- **Process** — ordered sequence of stages or activities.
- **Method** — structured repeatable approach for a defined objective.
- **Technique** — execution-level procedure used within a task, method, or process.
- **Distinction** — explicit conceptual separation between related or commonly conflated phenomena.
