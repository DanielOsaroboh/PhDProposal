I think organizing by **cognitive task** is much more powerful than organizing by **question type**. It mirrors how you actually think when learning, researching, and solving problems.

Instead of asking "Is this a definition question or a comparison question?", ask:

> **What am I trying to accomplish?**

That gives you a structure like this:

```text
13_Questions
│
├── 01_Learn
├── 02_Understand
├── 03_Explain
├── 04_Analyse
├── 05_Compare
├── 06_Evaluate
├── 07_Decide
├── 08_Explore
├── 09_Create
└── 10_Reflect
```

Now let's classify all of your questions.

---

# 01_Learn

**Goal:** Acquire foundational knowledge.

```text
01_Learn
│
├── What is Machine Learning?
├── What is an HPHT Reservoir?
├── What is Bayesian Optimisation?
```

These simply help you learn what something is.

---

# 02_Understand

**Goal:** Understand relationships and dependencies.

```text
02_Understand
│
├── How is Pressure related to Foam Rheology?
├── Which variables influence Fracture Conductivity?
├── Which models use Cross Validation?
├── What does Bayesian Optimisation require?
├── What prerequisites are needed for Gaussian Process Regression?
```

Notice these are about **connections**, not isolated facts.

## An even better idea

Since your entire knowledge system is built around **thinking**, I'd rename **13_Questions** to something broader, such as:

```text
13_Thinking
│
├── Learn
├── Understand
├── Explain
├── Analyse
├── Compare
├── Evaluate
├── Decide
├── Explore
├── Create
└── Reflect
```

This is more than a cosmetic change. You're not just collecting questions—you are organizing **thinking activities**. A note like **"Random Forest vs XGBoost"** is not valuable because it's a question; it's valuable because it helps you **compare**. Likewise, **"Which model should I choose?"** is valuable because it supports **decision-making**. Framing the folder around cognitive tasks rather than question forms makes the system more intuitive and aligns it with how you actually solve problems.
