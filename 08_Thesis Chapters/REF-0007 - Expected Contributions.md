# 8. Expected Contributions

This research is expected to contribute to petroleum engineering through the data-driven prediction and engineering assessment of nanoparticle-stabilised energised fracturing foams under HPHT conditions. The expected contributions are aligned with the research objectives and proposed methodology.

**8.1 Structured HPHT Foam Data Resource**

Through the systematic extraction, digitisation, harmonisation, and standardisation of available experimental data, the research is expected to produce a structured dataset and reproducible data-preparation approach for the analysis of heterogeneous HPHT foam rheology and stability data.

**8.2 Predictive Framework for HPHT Foam Rheology and Stability**

The study is expected to develop and evaluate a data-driven framework for predicting the apparent viscosity and foam stability of nanoparticle-stabilised energised foams across varying formulation and HPHT operating conditions.

**8.3 Uncertainty Quantification and Model Reliability Assessment**

The research is expected to extend deterministic prediction by quantifying predictive uncertainty and assessing model reliability under sparse and extrapolated HPHT conditions. This will provide a basis for interpreting model predictions with greater awareness of their confidence and limitations.

**8.4 Candidate Operating-Window Identification**

The study is expected to identify uncertainty-bounded candidate operating windows representing combinations of pressure, temperature, foam quality, salinity, surfactant concentration, and nanoparticle concentration associated with acceptable rheological and stability performance under defined engineering constraints.

**8.5 Engineering Assessment for Fracturing-Fluid Design**

The research is expected to evaluate the implications of predicted foam rheology, stability, and candidate operating windows for fracturing-fluid design and the downstream assessment of proppant suspension and transport. This assessment will connect the predictive outputs to relevant petroleum engineering considerations without treating the models as substitutes for experimental validation or engineering judgement.

Collectively, these contributions are expected to provide an uncertainty-aware predictive framework for estimating apparent viscosity and foam stability, quantifying prediction uncertainty, and identifying candidate operating windows for nanoparticle-stabilised energised foams under HPHT conditions. The resulting outputs are intended to support further laboratory and engineering evaluation rather than provide automatically field-ready design decisions.
