# THK-0028 - Predictive Uncertainty

## Definition

Uncertainty associated with predicted foam properties because of data variability, incomplete coverage, model limitations and unfamiliar conditions.

## Core Idea

It expresses how strongly the available evidence supports a predicted value.

## Why It Matters

Central bridge between prediction and defensible engineering interpretation.

### Prediction Uncertainty as an Engineering Issue

Predictive uncertainty becomes engineering-relevant when predicted foam
properties are used to select formulations or operating conditions.

The issue is therefore not only:

> What apparent viscosity or foam stability does the model predict?

It is also:

> How much confidence should be placed in that prediction under the
> specified HPHT conditions?

The decision chain is:

Foam-property prediction
        ↓
Prediction uncertainty
        ↓
Confidence in predicted performance
        ↓
Confidence in fluid-design decision
        ↓
Confidence in downstream engineering assessment

A prediction without information about its reliability can therefore
provide an incomplete basis for engineering decision-making.

This is particularly important where operating conditions differ from
those strongly represented in the available experimental data.

## Structure / Characteristics

Data uncertainty + model uncertainty + domain coverage → uncertainty around prediction.

## Governing Variables / Conditions

The object should be interpreted through the operating conditions, formulation variables, structural state, and engineering context represented in the connected notes.

## Engineering Application

Its practical meaning emerges through its effect on hydraulic-fracturing fluid design, performance assessment, or decision support.

## Boundaries

The concept is context-dependent; values, regimes, and performance implications should not be generalised beyond the supporting evidence without validation.

## Explicit Research Role

Predictive uncertainty is an explicit research output rather than a
secondary statistical consideration.

The research asks:

> How can prediction uncertainty be quantified and incorporated into
> the interpretation of foam-rheology models?

The intended analytical progression is:

Point Prediction
        ↓
Uncertainty Quantification
        ↓
Reliability Assessment
        ↓
Reliability-Aware Interpretation
        ↓
Engineering Use

Thus uncertainty becomes part of the interpretation of model output
rather than information reported separately from the engineering
problem.
