# THK-0061 - Foam Performance–Fracture Conductivity Link

## Relationship Definition

Foam rheology and stability can influence downstream fracture
performance through their effect on proppant suspension, transport,
and placement.

## Upstream Properties

Two important upstream properties are:

- [[THK-0005 - Foam Apparent Viscosity]];
- [[THK-0006 - Foam Stability]].

These properties influence the foam's ability to maintain useful
proppant-carrying behaviour.

## Engineering Chain

```text
Apparent viscosity
        +
Foam stability
        ↓
Proppant suspension
        ↓
Proppant transport
        ↓
Proppant placement / distribution
        ↓
Potential influence on fracture conductivity
````

## Mechanistic Interpretation

Fracture conductivity is not determined directly by foam viscosity  
alone.

Rather, foam performance contributes through a downstream chain in  
which rheology and stability affect the ability of the fluid to suspend  
and transport proppant.

## Important Boundary

This relationship should therefore not be interpreted as:

> Higher foam viscosity automatically produces higher fracture  
> conductivity.

Fracture conductivity depends on multiple additional factors, including  
proppant characteristics, concentration, placement, fracture geometry,  
closure stress, and reservoir conditions.

## Engineering Significance

The relationship demonstrates why prediction errors in primary foam  
properties can have consequences beyond rheological characterisation.

Errors can affect interpretation of the fluid's potential ability to  
support effective proppant placement.

## Research Relevance

The research does not need to claim direct prediction of fracture  
conductivity.

Instead, fracture conductivity represents a **downstream engineering  
consequence** that provides context for why reliable prediction of foam  
rheology and stability matters.

## Evidence

Tran et al. (2025) is cited in the research problem in connection with  
downstream engineering implications of foam rheological and stability  
performance.
