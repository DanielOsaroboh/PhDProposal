# THK-0041 - Proppant Transport

## Function Definition

The suspension, movement and distribution of proppant through a created fracture network.

## Engineering Objective

The fluid must carry solids sufficiently to support useful fracture conductivity.

## Functional Chain

```text
Fluid rheology/stability → suspension/settling → transport/distribution.
```

## Required Upstream Properties

Adequate rheology and foam stability are required to support suspension and movement of proppant.

### Dependence on Predicted Foam Performance

Proppant transport is a downstream engineering function of the foam
system.

Its assessment depends partly on the reliability with which upstream
foam properties are known.

Apparent viscosity
        +
Foam stability
        ↓
Proppant suspension
        ↓
Proppant transport and distribution
        ↓
Potential influence on fracture conductivity

Consequently, uncertainty in predicted apparent viscosity or foam
stability can propagate into uncertainty in subsequent assessments of
proppant behaviour.

This makes proppant transport relevant not only as an engineering
performance outcome but also as a downstream consequence of predictive
reliability.

**Evidence:** Tran et al. (2025).


## Performance Indicators

Relevant indicators include settling behaviour, suspension persistence, transport/distribution performance, and compatibility with fracture-flow conditions.

## Failure Conditions

Loss of viscosity or stability, especially under elevated temperature, can degrade the function.

## Research Relevance

Provides the downstream engineering purpose of maintaining useful foam properties.

## Evidence

Fei et al. (2018); Goel et al. (2002).
