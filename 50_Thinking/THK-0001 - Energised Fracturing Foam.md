# THK-0001 - Energised Fracturing Foam

## Definition

A multiphase hydraulic-fracturing fluid containing a liquid phase and a compressible gas phase, commonly nitrogen or carbon dioxide.

## Core Idea

Uses a substantial gas fraction to reduce liquid loading while retaining useful rheological behaviour for stimulation.

## Why It Matters

Foundation fluid system for the research.

### HPHT Engineering Context

In HPHT hydraulic fracturing, energised foams are used not only to reduce
water consumption but also to minimise formation-damage risks and support
proppant transport.

Their usefulness therefore depends on maintaining adequate rheological
and stability characteristics throughout changing reservoir and operating
conditions.

The engineering value of the foam is consequently conditional:

Energised foam
    ↓
Reduced water loading
    +
Potential reduction in formation damage
    +
Proppant-transport capability
    ↓
Only useful if rheology and stability remain adequate under HPHT conditions

This means that selection of an energised foam cannot be based solely on
its initial laboratory performance. Its behaviour must be evaluated across
the range of conditions relevant to the intended fracturing operation.

**Evidence:** Wanniarachchi et al. (2017); Fei et al. (2018).

## Structure / Characteristics

Liquid + gas + surfactant + optional stabilisers → foam structure → rheology/stability → fracturing performance.
