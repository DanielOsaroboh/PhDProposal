# THK-0110 - Published-Data-First Acquisition Strategy

## Definition

The published-data-first acquisition strategy is a research strategy in
which the primary empirical dataset is constructed initially from
accessible published experimental evidence rather than depending on
proprietary or uncertain external datasets.

## Core Principle

The research should be designed around data that can realistically be
obtained.

Therefore:

```text
Accessible Published Evidence
        ↓
Primary Data Foundation
        ↓
Public / Supplementary Data
        ↓
Optional Approved Research-Group Data
        ↓
Master Dataset
```


## Data-Source Hierarchy

### Primary Source

Published experimental datasets and measurements.

### Supplementary Sources

Publicly available datasets and supplementary research materials.

### Conditional Sources

Research-group datasets where they are:

- available;
- relevant;
- appropriately approved.

## What the Strategy Avoids

The feasibility of the PhD should not depend critically upon:

- proprietary datasets;
- inaccessible industrial data;
- data whose release is uncertain;
- research-group data that have not been approved.

## Why It Matters

A methodology dependent on unavailable data creates a feasibility risk.

The published-data-first strategy reduces this dependency by making  
accessible evidence the methodological starting point.

## Strategic Logic

```
Research Ambition
        ↓
Constrained by
        ↓
Realistically Accessible Evidence
        ↓
Feasible Research Design
```

## Important Boundary

This strategy does not exclude additional high-quality datasets.

It establishes that such datasets should strengthen the research rather  
than determine whether the research can proceed at all.

## Research Significance

The strategy makes data accessibility part of research design rather  
than treating it as a later logistical problem.
