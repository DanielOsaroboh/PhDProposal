# THK-0012 - Coupled HPHT Effects

## Phenomenon

The joint influence of pressure, temperature and other operating/formulation variables on foam behaviour.

## Starting Condition

One-factor effects may not represent responses produced when variables act simultaneously.

## Causal Chain

```text
P × T × foam quality × shear × salinity × formulation → nonlinear joint response.
```

### Expanded Coupling Structure

The coupled HPHT problem extends beyond pressure–temperature interaction.

The response may involve simultaneous interaction among:

Pressure × Temperature × Foam Quality × Surfactant Chemistry
× Nanoparticle Concentration × Salinity × Shear Rate
× Gas-Phase Characteristics
        ↓
Foam microstructure and physicochemical processes
        ↓
Apparent viscosity + foam stability

This means that changing one variable can alter the response associated
with another variable.

The engineering implication is important:

> Performance observed under one combination of variables does not
> necessarily establish performance under another combination.

This creates a multidimensional prediction problem rather than a series
of independent one-variable prediction problems.

**Evidence:** Al-Darweesh et al. (2024); Ghorbani (2025).

## Why the Mechanism Matters

Supports multivariable modelling and interaction-aware interpretation.

## Governing Variables

The strength and direction of the mechanism can depend on pressure, temperature, shear, foam quality, salinity, concentration, and formulation chemistry as relevant.

## Observable Outcome

The mechanism becomes visible through changes in foam structure, apparent viscosity, stability, drainage, or downstream engineering behaviour.

## Boundary Conditions

The causal pathway should not be assumed to have identical strength across formulations or outside tested operating ranges.

## Evidence

Sinha et al. (2019).
