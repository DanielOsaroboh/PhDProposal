# THK-0062 - Prediction Reliability–Treatment Performance Link

## Relationship Definition

The reliability of foam-property predictions can influence confidence
in downstream assessments of hydraulic-fracturing treatment
performance.

This relationship operates indirectly through a chain of engineering
dependencies.

## Upstream Prediction

The primary predicted properties are:

- [[THK-0005 - Foam Apparent Viscosity]];
- [[THK-0006 - Foam Stability]].

Their usefulness depends partly on:

- [[THK-0028 - Predictive Uncertainty]];
- [[THK-0051 - Model Reliability under Unfamiliar Conditions]].

## Engineering Relationship

```text
Predicted viscosity + stability
        ↓
Prediction reliability
        ↓
Fluid-design confidence
        ↓
Proppant suspension assessment
        ↓
Proppant transport assessment
        ↓
Fracture-performance interpretation
        ↓
Treatment-performance confidence
````

## Uncertainty Pathway

The same chain can be expressed from the uncertainty perspective:

```text
Foam-property uncertainty
        ↓
Fluid-design uncertainty
        ↓
Proppant-behaviour uncertainty
        ↓
Fracture-performance uncertainty
        ↓
Treatment-assessment uncertainty
```

## Key Distinction

This does **not** mean that predictive uncertainty in foam rheology  
directly determines treatment success.

Treatment performance depends on many additional reservoir,  
geomechanical, operational, and completion variables.

The relationship is therefore:

> **an uncertainty-propagation pathway**

rather than:

> **a direct deterministic relationship.**

## Engineering Significance

This distinction prevents overclaiming while preserving the practical  
importance of prediction reliability.

The research can legitimately argue that unreliable predictions of  
primary foam properties reduce confidence in subsequent engineering  
assessment without claiming that the proposed model directly predicts  
overall treatment success.

## Decision Implication

Engineering interpretation should therefore consider:

1. predicted property values;
    
2. uncertainty associated with those values;
    
3. whether conditions are represented by available data;
    
4. downstream engineering relevance;
    
5. need for further laboratory or engineering evaluation.
    

## Research Relevance

This relationship establishes the outer engineering boundary of the  
proposed predictive framework.

The framework supports engineering assessment but does not replace  
full hydraulic-fracturing design or field-performance modelling.

## Evidence

Tran et al. (2025) is cited in the research problem for the downstream  
engineering implications associated with foam performance.
