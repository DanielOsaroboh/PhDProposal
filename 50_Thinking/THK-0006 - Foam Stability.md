# THK-0006 - Foam Stability

## Definition

The ability of a foam to preserve its structure and resist drainage and destabilisation over a relevant operational period.

## What It Represents

Stability determines whether useful foam properties persist during preparation, pumping, fracture propagation and proppant placement.

## Why It Matters

A primary performance target alongside apparent viscosity.

## Governing Variables

Interfacial strength + drainage resistance → structural persistence → sustained engineering function.

### HPHT Destabilisation Processes

Foam stability under HPHT conditions is influenced by several
physicochemical processes.

Important processes include:

- liquid drainage;
- bubble coalescence;
- gas diffusion;
- temperature-dependent interfacial behaviour;
- pressure-dependent gas-phase behaviour.

A simplified degradation pathway is:

HPHT conditions
    ↓
Changes in gas and liquid-phase behaviour
    ↓
Liquid drainage / gas diffusion / bubble interaction
    ↓
Foam-structure evolution
    ↓
Change in foam stability

Consequently, foam stability should be regarded as a dynamic property
that emerges from interacting physicochemical processes rather than as
a fixed characteristic of a formulation.

**Evidence:** Gonzalez Perdomo and Wan Madihi (2022).

## Measurement / Interpretation

The property should be interpreted at explicitly stated pressure, temperature, shear, foam quality, salinity, and formulation conditions where applicable.

## Engineering Significance

Changes in this property can alter downstream suspension, transport, or operating-window assessment.

## Boundaries

A measured or predicted value is meaningful only within its experimental or model domain and associated uncertainty.
