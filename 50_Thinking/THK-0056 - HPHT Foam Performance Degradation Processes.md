# THK-0056 - HPHT Foam Performance Degradation Processes

## Phenomenon

HPHT conditions can initiate or accelerate physicochemical processes
that alter the structure, rheology, and stability of energised foams.

These degradation processes help explain why foam performance observed
under less demanding conditions may not persist under HPHT conditions.

## Starting Condition

The mechanism begins when an energised foam is exposed to elevated
pressure and temperature during hydraulic-fracturing operations.

The resulting environment can influence:

- liquid drainage;
- bubble coalescence;
- gas diffusion;
- bubble structure;
- interfacial behaviour;
- gas-phase characteristics.

## Causal Chain


```text
HPHT Conditions
        ↓
Changes in physicochemical behaviour
        ↓
Liquid drainage
+ Bubble coalescence
+ Gas diffusion
+ Interfacial changes
        ↓
Foam-structure evolution
        ↓
Changes in apparent viscosity
+ Changes in foam stability
        ↓
Potential degradation of engineering performance
```


## Principal Degradation Processes

### Liquid Drainage

Liquid can migrate through the foam structure, reducing the liquid  
content of foam films and altering structural stability.

### Bubble Coalescence

Loss of separation between neighbouring bubbles can produce larger  
bubbles and reduce structural integrity.

### Gas Diffusion

Gas transfer between bubbles can change bubble-size distribution and  
foam structure.

### Interfacial Change

Temperature, pressure, surfactants, and nanoparticles can influence  
the behaviour of the gas–liquid interface.

## Governing Conditions

The magnitude of degradation depends on more than temperature and  
pressure alone.

Relevant interacting conditions include:

- [[THK-0008 - HPHT Conditions]]
- [[THK-0015 - Foam Quality]]
- surfactant chemistry;
- nanoparticle concentration;
- salinity;
- [[THK-0011 - Shear Effect on Foam Behaviour]];
- gas-phase characteristics.

## Observable Outcomes

Performance degradation may appear through:

- reduced [[THK-0006 - Foam Stability]];
- changes in [[THK-0005 - Foam Apparent Viscosity]];
- accelerated drainage;
- structural changes;
- reduced proppant-suspension capability.

## Engineering Significance

The mechanism establishes an important chain:

HPHT exposure  
→ physicochemical change  
→ foam-property change  
→ engineering-performance change.

Therefore, predicting foam performance requires representation of the  
conditions that govern these degradation processes.

## Boundary Conditions

The existence of these processes does not imply that every foam will  
degrade identically.

Their magnitude depends on formulation, gas phase, operating conditions,  
and experimental domain.

## Evidence

Gonzalez Perdomo and Wan Madihi (2022) identify physicochemical  
processes associated with changes in foam rheology and stability under  
elevated pressure and temperature.
