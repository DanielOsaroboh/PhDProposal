# THK-0069 - Uncertainty Quantification in Petroleum Engineering

## Definition

Uncertainty quantification is the systematic characterisation of
uncertainty associated with model inputs, predictions, parameters, or
engineering outcomes.

## Core Idea

Engineering prediction should distinguish between:

> **the predicted value**

and

> **the uncertainty associated with that prediction.**

A model may predict a particular outcome while the available evidence
supports that prediction with different degrees of confidence.

## General Structure

```text
Data + Model + Assumptions
        ↓
Prediction
        +
Uncertainty Assessment
        ↓
Reliability Information
        ↓
Risk-Aware Engineering Interpretation

```

## Petroleum-Engineering Context

Uncertainty quantification has been applied in petroleum-engineering  
areas including:

- reservoir characterisation;
- production forecasting.

These applications demonstrate that uncertainty can be incorporated  
into predictive engineering analysis rather than treating predictions  
as perfectly certain.

## Sources of Uncertainty

Relevant sources can include:

- experimental variability;
- sparse data;
- parameter uncertainty;
- model limitations;
- incomplete domain coverage;
- extrapolation.

## Why It Matters

Two predictions with similar numerical values may have very different  
levels of empirical support.

Therefore:

```
Prediction A = value x
Prediction B = value x

does not necessarily imply

Reliability A = Reliability B
```

## Engineering Significance

Uncertainty information can help engineers distinguish:

- well-supported predictions;
- uncertain predictions;
- conditions requiring caution;
- conditions requiring additional evaluation.

## Research Relevance

The significance for this PhD lies in transferring the logic of  
uncertainty-aware prediction into nanoparticle-stabilised energised-foam  
rheology.
