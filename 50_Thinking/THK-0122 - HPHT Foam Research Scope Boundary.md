# THK-0122 - HPHT Foam Research Scope Boundary

## Definition

The HPHT foam research scope boundary defines the domain within which
the doctoral research develops, evaluates, and interprets its
predictive framework.

## Core Scope

The research is bounded to:

> evaluation and prediction of nanoparticle-stabilised energised
> fracturing foams under HPHT conditions.

## Primary Predictive Scope

The central predictive targets are:

- [[THK-0005 - Foam Apparent Viscosity]]
- [[THK-0006 - Foam Stability]]

Foam stability is operationalised primarily through:

- [[THK-0124 - Foam Half-Life as Stability Operationalisation]]

## Downstream Engineering Scope

Predicted rheology and stability are subsequently used to inform:

- [[THK-0041 - Proppant Transport]]
- [[THK-0103 - Uncertainty-Bounded Operating Window]]

These are downstream engineering applications rather than equivalent
primary predictive targets.

## Scope Architecture

```text
Nanoparticle-Stabilised Energised Foam
        ↓
HPHT Conditions
        ↓
PRIMARY PREDICTIVE SCOPE
        │
        ├── Apparent Viscosity
        └── Foam Stability
                 ↓
        Predictive Uncertainty
                 ↓
DOWNSTREAM ENGINEERING APPLICATIONS
        │
        ├── Candidate Operating Windows
        └── Proppant Suspension / Transport Assessment
````

## Explicit Exclusion

The research does not attempt to develop a fully coupled  
three-dimensional hydraulic-fracturing simulator.

See:

[[THK-0125 - Hydraulic-Fracturing Simulation Exclusion Boundary]]

## Intended Engineering Role

The framework is intended to support:

- further laboratory evaluation;
- engineering assessment;
- existing fracture-design workflows.

It is therefore a decision-support framework rather than a complete  
fracture-design system.

## Empirical Boundary

The scope is additionally constrained by the characteristics of  
accessible HPHT foam data, including:

- availability;
- dataset size;
- heterogeneity;
- variable coverage;
- formulation specificity.

## Boundary Principle

The scope can therefore be represented as:

```
Research Objectives
        +
Available Physical Evidence
        +
Defined Predictive Targets
        +
Engineering Application Boundary
        ↓
Permissible Research Scope
```

## Why the Boundary Matters

Explicit scope prevents downstream engineering implications from being  
mistaken for direct predictive claims.

In particular:

Prediction of Foam Rheology  
≠  
Prediction of Complete Fracturing Performance

and:

Candidate Operating Window  
≠  
Field-Validated Operating Envelope
