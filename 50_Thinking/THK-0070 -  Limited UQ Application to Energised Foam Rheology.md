# THK-0070 - Limited UQ Application to Energised Foam Rheology

## Gap Statement

Although uncertainty quantification has been applied in other areas of
petroleum engineering, its integration with predictive modelling of
nanoparticle-stabilised energised-foam rheology remains limited.

## What Is Known

Uncertainty-aware approaches have been used in petroleum-engineering
applications such as:

- reservoir characterisation;
- production forecasting.

At the same time, machine-learning methods have been applied to foam
rheological prediction.

## What Is Missing

These two research streams remain insufficiently integrated for the
specific problem of HPHT nanoparticle-stabilised energised foam.

```text
Petroleum Engineering UQ
        │
        │ established elsewhere
        ↓
Reservoir / Production Applications

Foam Machine Learning
        │
        │ developed separately
        ↓
Rheological Prediction

                ↓

Missing Integration

Foam Prediction
        +
Explicit Predictive Uncertainty

```

## Why Existing Prediction Is Insufficient

A deterministic foam model can produce an apparent-viscosity or  
stability prediction without indicating how reliable that prediction  
is under:

- sparse conditions;
- unfamiliar conditions;
- variable experimental observations;
- extrapolated conditions.

## Engineering Consequence

Without uncertainty information, engineers have limited information  
about whether a candidate prediction is strongly or weakly supported by  
the available evidence.

This affects confidence in:

- formulation assessment;
- operating-range identification;
- proppant-performance interpretation;
- subsequent engineering evaluation.

## Research Need

The required development is therefore not simply:

> another foam-prediction model.

It is:

> **a foam-prediction approach that explicitly incorporates prediction  
> reliability and uncertainty.**

## Contribution Opportunity

The gap creates an opportunity to connect established uncertainty  
principles from petroleum engineering with nonlinear prediction of  
HPHT energised-foam behaviour.

## Evidence

Ogbidi and Oteh (2023) and Zhang et al. (2025) demonstrate uncertainty  
applications in other petroleum-engineering contexts.

The reviewed literature indicates limited integration with  
nanoparticle-stabilised energised-foam rheology.
